"We need our metal detector calibrated." We hear it all the time, and you'll find plenty of companies offering "metal detector calibration." It's an understandable way to put it. But the word doesn't quite fit a metal detector, and the difference matters when an auditor reviews your records.

Here's what calibration, validation, and verification really mean, which one your metal detector needs, and how to make sure you get the right documentation, whatever the service is called.

## Three terms that get mixed up

| Term | What it means | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| **Calibration** | Comparing a measuring device against a traceable reference standard and documenting its error. Adjusting the device is a separate, optional step. | A thermometer read side by side with a reference thermometer at the ice point |
| **Verification** | Confirming a device or control meets a stated requirement: pass or fail. | Your operators' start-up check with test pieces |
| **Validation** | Proving a process or control achieves its intended purpose. | Proving your metal detector reliably finds and rejects the specified test pieces in your real product |

## Why "calibration" doesn't really fit a metal detector

Calibration is for **measuring** instruments: devices that give you a number, like a thermometer, a hygrometer, or a scale. You compare that number with a reference, record the error, and decide whether it's within tolerance.

A metal detector doesn't measure anything you can compare that way. Its job is to make a decision: metal or no metal, reject or accept. What matters is whether it **detects and rejects** contamination of a given size in your product, at your line speed, every time. Proving that is validation.

Part of the confusion is that many detectors have a setup routine, often called "product learn" or even "calibrate," that tunes the detector to a product. That step is useful, but it's setup, not proof. On its own, it doesn't show an auditor that the detector finds the test pieces your plan requires.

## What auditors actually look for

Food safety codes such as SQF and BRCGS expect you to show that your foreign material controls work, not just that a technician visited. For a metal detector, an auditor typically wants to see:

- **The sizes** of ferrous, non-ferrous, and stainless steel test pieces your plan requires, and why
- **Proof the detector finds them** in your actual products, at the leading edge, center, and trailing edge
- **Proof contaminated product is rejected**, and that the reject system and fail-safes work
- **Certified test pieces**, traceable to national standards
- **Routine verification records** from your own team between validations
- **What happened when something failed**, and what was changed

A certificate that just says "calibrated" without this detail can leave you exposed at audit time.

## So what does a "metal detector calibration" service usually include?

When a provider sells metal detector calibration, it often includes running test pieces through the detector and adjusting the settings if it misses them. In practice that's validation plus adjustment, which is fine. What matters is what's in the report. Whatever it's called, make sure you get:

- Test piece sizes, metal types, and certificate numbers
- The products tested and the positions each piece passed through
- Pass results for every test piece, in every position
- Reject system and fail-safe results
- **As-found and as-left settings** if anything was adjusted
- The date, the technician, and a clear next-due date

If the paperwork doesn't show these, ask for them before your next audit.

## Where calibration is the right word

Calibration is exactly the right term for the instruments that monitor your process: **thermometers, temperature probes, fixed temperature gauges, and thermohygrometers**. We calibrate those against NIST-traceable reference standards and record the error at each point, with a pass or fail against your tolerance. Adjusting the device is only needed when it's out of tolerance. See our [thermometer and hygrometer calibration](/services/thermometer-hygrometer-calibration) service.

## The bottom line

Metal detectors are **validated**, and adjusted when needed. Thermometers and hygrometers are **calibrated**. Both get **verified** against a requirement. Whatever your auditor, your customer, or your current provider calls it, what protects you is a clear, complete record that your equipment works.
