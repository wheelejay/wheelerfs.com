Passing your SQF or BRCGS audit isn't always enough. Many large retailers and brand owners have their **own requirements for metal detection and X-ray inspection**, and they can be stricter than your certification scheme. If you supply one of them, their rules are part of your food safety program.

## Retailers can set their own rules

Big-box retailers, club stores, grocery chains, and brand owners often publish supplier food safety requirements or audit expectations. For foreign material detection, these can include:

- **Which technology** you must use, metal detection or X-ray, for which products
- **Maximum test piece sizes** for ferrous, non-ferrous, and stainless steel
- **How often** you check the equipment, and what records you keep
- **Reject system features**, such as automatic rejects, reject confirmation, and locked bins
- **Fail-safe requirements**, such as alarms and line stops when something goes wrong
- **Validation** by a qualified person, and how often

## Costco and X-ray

Costco is a frequent topic among QA managers. Its supplier food safety audit expectations have been reported to require a **properly installed X-ray detection device for finished product**, with limited exceptions, and to still require metal detection for products that aren't suited to X-ray. Requirements change, so **always check the current version of the document Costco gives you**, and ask your buyer or Costco's food safety team about anything unclear.

Other retailers have their own rules, and some refer to industry codes of practice that spell out test methods in detail, including testing at the leading edge, center, and trailing edge of the product.

## How to stay compliant

1. **Get the current requirements** from every retailer and brand owner you supply, and keep them on file.
2. **Compare them with your food safety plan**: technology, test piece sizes, check frequencies, reject features, and fail-safes. The strictest requirement wins.
3. **Update your plan and procedures** to match, and train your operators.
4. **Make sure your equipment can meet it.** If a retailer requires X-ray, or smaller test pieces than your detector can reliably find, plan for it early.
5. **Validate against the retailer's requirements**, using their test piece sizes, in your real products.
6. **Keep records** your retailer's auditor can follow: verification logs, corrective actions, and validation certificates.
