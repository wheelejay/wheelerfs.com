A metal detector that keeps rejecting good product is expensive and frustrating. Worse, it tempts people to do the one thing they shouldn't: **turn down the sensitivity** until the rejects stop. That can let real metal through.

Here are the most common causes of false rejects in food plants, and how to fix them without weakening your food safety control.

## 1. Product effect

Wet, salty, or conductive products, like meat, cheese, sauces, brined foods, and some supplements, create their own signal as they pass through the detector. If the detector isn't set up for that product, it can mistake the product for metal.

**Things that change product effect:**

- A **new formulation** or ingredient
- **Product temperature**, especially frozen product that partly thaws
- **Pack size or orientation**
- **Packaging**, especially metallized film

**Fixes:** re-learn the product on the detector, use a **separate product program** for each product that behaves differently, control product temperature, and **revalidate** after any change.

## 2. The wrong product program

After a changeover, the detector may still be running the last product's settings. That can cause false rejects, or worse, missed metal.

**Fix:** make program selection part of every changeover checklist, and verify it at the changeover check.

## 3. Electrical interference

Nearby motors, variable-speed drives, radios, and other electrical equipment can create noise the detector picks up.

**Fix:** keep the metal-free zone clear, check grounding and cable routing, and have the manufacturer or a service technician investigate persistent noise.

## 4. Vibration and mechanical issues

Vibration from the conveyor or nearby equipment, loose parts, or metal moving within the detector's metal-free zone can all trigger rejects.

**Fix:** check the mounting, belt, rollers, and frame, and keep the metal-free zone free of moving metal.

## 5. Static and grounding (gravity and pipeline systems)

Powders and granules falling through a gravity system can build up static. Ungrounded pipework can cause false signals.

**Fix:** check grounding and pipe connections, and talk to the manufacturer about static control.

## 6. Damaged or worn equipment

A damaged detector head, failing electronics, or worn belt joints can cause erratic behavior.

**Fix:** get the equipment serviced by the manufacturer or a qualified technician, then revalidate.

## What not to do

- **Don't lower the sensitivity** to stop false rejects without a documented reason and a revalidation. You may stop the false rejects and start missing metal.
- **Don't ignore the rejects.** Investigate them. Some "false" rejects turn out to be real metal.
- **Don't bypass the reject system.**

## Revalidate after the fix

Any change to settings, programs, or equipment to fix false rejects should be followed by a **revalidation**: certified test pieces, in your product, at the hardest positions, with reject and fail-safe checks. That's your proof the fix didn't weaken the control.
