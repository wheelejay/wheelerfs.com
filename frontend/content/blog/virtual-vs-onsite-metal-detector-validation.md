Remote and virtual services have spread into food safety, and metal detector validation is no exception. Some providers now offer **virtual validation**: your team runs the tests while a technician watches over video, or you send in records and footage for review. It sounds convenient. But does it hold up when an auditor asks how you know your metal detector works?

Here's how virtual and on-site validation compare.

## What virtual validation usually looks like

Formats vary, but typically:

- You send an equipment list and your test piece specifications
- **Your own staff** run the test pieces through the detector, often on a video call
- The provider reviews the results and issues a report

The technician never touches the detector. Everything depends on what your team does, and what the camera shows.

## What on-site validation looks like

A technician comes to your plant and:

- Brings **certified test pieces** of known size and condition
- Runs them **in your actual products**, at the leading edge, center, and trailing edge, at line speed
- Confirms each one is **detected and rejected** into the locked bin
- Tests every **fail-safe**: reject confirmation, bin full, air pressure, and fault alarms
- **Adjusts the detector** and retests if something fails, recording as-found and as-left settings
- Issues a signed certificate for each unit

## How they compare

| | Virtual | On-site |
|---|---|---|
| **Who runs the tests** | Your staff | An independent technician |
| **Test pieces** | Usually yours | Certified pieces the technician brings |
| **Independence** | Limited: it relies on your team's testing | Independent, third-party testing |
| **Fail-safe testing** | Depends on what can be shown on camera | Tested hands-on |
| **If something fails** | Usually a recommendation | Adjusted and retested on the spot |
| **Plant time** | Your staff's time to run the tests | About an hour per detector, with your staff mostly free |
| **Audit acceptance** | Varies by auditor and scheme | Widely accepted |

## What do auditors think?

There's no single answer. On industry forums, QA managers regularly ask whether virtual metal detector validation is accepted for SQF audits, and experiences differ by auditor and certification body. What auditors consistently look for is **independent evidence** that the detector meets your critical limits in your products, and that the reject system and fail-safes work. The harder that is to show from a video, the more likely you'll get questions.

If you're considering virtual validation, **ask your certification body** first, and check your customers' requirements. Some specifically expect third-party, on-site testing.

## When virtual might be enough

- A quick check between full validations
- Remote sites where no local provider is available
- Training your team on how to run checks
