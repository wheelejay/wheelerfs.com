## Your metal detector as a HACCP critical control point

When metal is a hazard in your plan, your metal detector is usually the step that controls it, making it a critical control point under HACCP or a process preventive control under FDA's Preventive Controls rule. Either way, your plan needs:

- **A critical limit**: the ferrous, non-ferrous, and stainless steel test piece sizes the detector must find and reject in your product
- **Monitoring**: your operators' routine checks with test pieces at start-up, during production, and at changeovers
- **Corrective actions**: what happens to product when a check fails
- **Verification and validation**: proof the detector meets the critical limit in your real products, with records

Our validation gives you that verification and validation evidence: every test piece, every product, every position, the reject system, and the fail-safes, documented on one certificate per detector.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does Utah require metal detectors in food plants?

Utah doesn't have its own metal detection law. Utah food plants follow federal rules: FDA's Preventive Controls for Human Food rule (21 CFR Part 117), or USDA FSIS HACCP rules (9 CFR Part 417) for meat and poultry, plus GFSI schemes like SQF and BRCGS and their customers' requirements. If metal is a hazard in your hazard analysis, you need a control for it, and for most plants that's a metal detector or X-ray system that is monitored, verified, and validated.

### Is a metal detector a critical control point?

Often, yes. If the metal detector is the step that removes metal from your product, it's usually a CCP in a HACCP plan, or a process preventive control under FDA's rule. Your plan should name the test piece sizes it must detect, how often operators check it, and what happens when a check fails.

### What's the difference between metal detector calibration and validation?

Calibration compares a measuring instrument with a reference standard. A metal detector doesn't give a reading to compare, so what auditors want is validation: proof it detects and rejects the specified test pieces in your product. Our validation includes adjusting (calibrating) the detector's settings when needed.

### How often should a metal detector be validated?

At least once a year, and again after installation, relocation, major repairs, or new products. Your operators also check it routinely during production. See [How Often Should You Validate Your Metal Detector?](/blog/how-often-validate-metal-detector)

### Do you work on all brands of industrial metal detectors?

Yes. Validation tests the detector's performance with certified test pieces in your product, so it works with any manufacturer's conveyor, gravity-fall, or pipeline system. Any adjustments follow the manufacturer's manual.

### How long does a metal detector validation take?

About an hour per detector, longer if a line runs several products or a detector needs adjusting.
