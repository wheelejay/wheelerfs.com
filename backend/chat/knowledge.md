### Thermometer & Hygrometer Calibration
Page: https://wheelerfs.com/services/thermometer-hygrometer-calibration
- NIST-traceable calibration of thermohygrometers (temperature + humidity
  meters, e.g. in dry storage), removable temperature probes, fixed or
  installed temperature gauges, and fridge, freezer, or room thermometers.
- Calibration means comparing the device against a traceable reference
  standard and documenting its error. It does not require adjusting the
  device; adjustment is a separate, optional step. Each point also gets a
  pass/fail against the manufacturer's (or the customer's) tolerance, which
  covers verification.
- Use "calibration" for these instruments and "validation" for metal
  detectors, X-ray systems, and magnets.
- Thermohygrometers: checked at two humidity points (ambient and about 75 %RH,
  in a sealed salt-solution chamber), with temperature at each point.
- Removable probes: ice point (0 °C / 32 °F) plus the customer's operating
  temperature. Fixed gauges: in place, at the equipment's operating
  temperature. Fridge, freezer, and room thermometers: in place.
- If a device can be adjusted and is out of tolerance, Jordan can adjust it and
  re-check it; the certificate then shows both as-found and as-left readings.
- Each device gets a Certificate of Calibration with a QR code, in the
  customer portal.
- Wheeler Food Safety's reference standards are calibrated by an accredited
  lab and are NIST-traceable. Do not say Wheeler itself is ISO 17025 or A2LA
  accredited, and don't call it an accredited calibration.
- Prices (first device of that kind / each additional device of the same kind
  on the same visit):
  - Thermohygrometer (2 points, RH + temp): $195 / $60
  - Temperature probe, removable (ice point + operating point): $175 / $90
  - Temperature gauge, fixed (in place, at operating temperature): $175 / $90
  - Fridge, freezer, or room thermometer: $125 / $40

## How a metal detector or X-ray validation visit works
- Time on site: about an hour per metal detector. Several products, or a
  detector that needs adjusting, can take longer; X-ray systems vary. Jordan
  confirms a time estimate when scheduling.
- Before testing: confirm certified, in-calibration test pieces that match the
  customer's sensitivity specification; review the specification, equipment
  settings, and the audit standard (for example SQF or BRCGS); record the
  as-found settings.
- Challenge testing: ferrous, non-ferrous, and stainless steel (316) test
  pieces, each run at the leading edge, center, and trailing edge of the
  product, and each must be detected and rejected three times in a row in
  every position. Testing uses the customer's real products (full pack, light
  pack, and different SKUs where they apply).
- Fail-safe checks: each fitted fail-safe is tested to confirm the system
  alarms or stops the line, for example reject confirmation, reject bin full,
  bin opened or unlocked, low air pressure, detector fault or power loss, and
  a failed timing sensor. Each is recorded as pass, fail, or not fitted.
- Pass criteria: 100% detection and proper rejection of every test piece in
  every position, false rejects within the customer's tolerance, and every
  fitted fail-safe working.
- If something fails: Jordan tells the customer right away, records the
  as-found condition, and with the customer's approval adjusts and calibrates
  the system per the manufacturer's manual, retesting until it passes. As-left
  settings and every adjustment are recorded. Problems that need a repair or
  parts are documented with a recommended fix and retested after the repair.
- Report: a signed PDF report per unit with equipment details, as-found and
  as-left settings, adjustments, test piece sizes, results, and fail-safe
  results. Wheeler Food Safety keeps copies for 3 years.
- To prepare: power at each unit, product available to run (or a simulated
  run), the QA or food safety manager available during the visit, site rules
  and PPE requirements shared in advance, and the sensitivity specification on
  hand.

## General guidance customers often ask about
- Equipment is typically validated at least once a year, and again after
  installation or relocation, major repairs or software updates, new products
  or packaging, line speed changes, or when a customer or certification body
  requires it.
- Annual validation is different from the operators' daily verification checks
  (start-up, during production, shutdown), which the plant does itself.
