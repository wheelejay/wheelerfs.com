Frequently asked questions

Is an X-ray system a critical control point?

Often, yes. If X-ray inspection is the step that removes metal, glass, stone, or bone from your product, it's usually a CCP in your HACCP plan, or a process preventive control under FDA's Preventive Controls rule (21 CFR Part 117). Your plan should name the contaminants and sizes it must detect, how often operators check it, and what happens when a check fails. Validation is the evidence that it meets those limits.

Does X-ray validation include radiation safety testing?

No. Validation proves food safety performance: detecting and rejecting contaminants in your product. Radiation safety, such as state registration, radiation surveys, and safety interlock checks, is separate and handled through your equipment supplier or a radiation safety provider.

Can you validate detection of glass, stone, or bone?

Yes. Most plants use X-ray mainly for metal, and we test with certified ferrous, non-ferrous, and stainless steel test pieces. If your plan names glass, stone, or calcified bone, we'll source the certified test standards your program requires.

How often should an X-ray system be validated?

At least once a year, and again when the system is installed, moved, or serviced, or when you add a new product or packaging format.

Is X-ray better than a metal detector?

It depends on your product and packaging. X-ray handles foil packaging and non-metal hazards; metal detectors are reliable and cost less for metal in most products. See Metal Detector vs. X-Ray Inspection.
